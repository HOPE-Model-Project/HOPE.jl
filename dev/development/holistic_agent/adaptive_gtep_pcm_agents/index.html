Adaptive Holistic Assessment Agents

Status and purpose

This document proposes an adaptive holistic-assessment framework for HOPE-AI after the validated deterministic GTEP-to-PCM composite tool. GTEP, realization, and PCM form the first concrete workflow, not the limit of the framework. This is a design specification, not an implemented agent system.

The motivating study pattern is iterative:

  1. GTEP produces a zonal capacity plan using planning abstractions such as a planning reserve margin.
  2. A realization process converts that plan into one or more nodal physical systems suitable for PCM.
  3. PCM tests chronological operation at higher spatial and temporal resolution.
  4. An independent verifier determines whether the evidence satisfies a predeclared HolisticAssessmentContract.
  5. If it does not, the responsible layer proposes a traceable revision. The workflow repeats until it passes, reaches an approved stopping condition, or returns to a human decision maker.

The existing deterministic linkage remains the computational foundation. It provides identities, fingerprints, immutable artifacts, conservation checks, and provenance. Agents add scientific diagnosis and controlled revision; they do not replace those contracts or move numerical data through conversation.

General holistic-assessment framework

A holistic assessment connects an upstream decision or design model to one or more higher-resolution realizations and assessment models, then returns evidence-based feedback when the proposed system cannot be constructed, solved, or accepted. The common framework is:

Study specification
        -> upstream decision model
        -> physical or semantic realization
        -> one or more assessment models
        -> assessment-specific verifier
        -> accept, revise, escalate, or stop

Reliability assessment is one important use case, but it is not the definition of holistic assessment. Other workflows may evaluate:

Assessment familyExample downstream evidence
Model and operational feasibilityModel construction, mathematical feasibility, commitment and dispatch consistency, or an explainable infeasibility diagnosis
Reliability and adequacyUnserved energy, reserve shortfall, contingencies, LOLE, EUE, or other approved deterministic or probabilistic metrics
Economics and deliverabilityProduction cost, congestion, curtailment, transmission utilization, uplift, or stranded capacity
Policy and emissionsRPS or clean-energy compliance, emissions limits, carbon cost, or technology constraints
Market performanceDay-ahead and real-time feasibility, reserve procurement, price formation, deviations, or settlement outcomes through DART
Resilience and stress testingPerformance under approved weather, outage, fuel, transmission, or demand stress scenarios

Each assessment family supplies its own versioned criteria and evaluator while reusing the same artifact lineage, finding, revision, approval, and stopping contracts. A study may compose several assessment modules, but each pass or fail claim must name its metric, horizon, threshold, and authoritative evidence.

Central design decision

A zonal GTEP solution does not generally imply one uniquely correct nodal PCM system. Downscaling must be treated as a constrained scientific realization problem with explicit data, objectives, and uncertainty.

The realization layer should support two policy families:

  • Declared realization: a case supplies known candidate locations, as in the current explicit_bus Maryland benchmark. The linkage verifies and applies the declaration deterministically.
  • Computed realization: a versioned method allocates zonal builds among eligible physical sites or buses. The method may produce several diverse, defensible realizations when the evidence does not identify one unique answer.

Both policies must be reproducible. Given the same parent artifacts, method version, parameters, solver settings, and tie-breaking rule, the same realization artifacts should be produced within declared numerical tolerances. This is determinism at the workflow and data-contract level; it does not imply that the physical siting assumption is uniquely true.

The scientific roles and workflow-governance roles are separate dimensions. A GTEP specialist can still operate through a centralized, durable workflow, and the verifier must remain independent of the agent that proposed a change.

Deterministic realization engine

The realization agent should not invent bus assignments in prose. It should construct or select a versioned RealizationPolicy, call a deterministic realization engine, inspect its evidence, and explain or revise the policy.

Required inputs

ArtifactRequired content
CapacityPlanGTEP run and input hashes; zonal build, retirement, storage, and transmission decisions; planning assumptions and units
ReferenceNetworkStable bus, branch, zone, voltage, rating, and existing-asset identities with data provenance
CandidateSiteCatalogCandidate site or bus ID, technology eligibility, zone, maximum MW/MWh, interconnection limit and cost, resource quality, exclusions, evidence, and confidence
OperationalTemplateRegistryUnit sizes, minimum output, ramping, minimum up/down time, outage, startup, reserve, emissions, and storage parameters by reviewed technology template
RealizationPolicyMethod and version, objective terms, constraints, weights, unitization rules, diversity settings, solver, tolerances, and deterministic tie-breaker
HolisticAssessmentContractSelected assessment modules, model-feasibility and assessment gates, evaluation horizons, allowed revisions, approvals, iteration limit, and compute budget

Constrained allocation

A first computed realization method can minimize reviewed siting, interconnection, network-stress, fragmentation, and assumption penalties subject to constraints such as:

  • exact conservation of selected zonal generation MW and storage MW/MWh;
  • technology and zone eligibility;
  • candidate-site and interconnection capacity;
  • discrete or bounded unitization where required;
  • stable physical identities and valid network references;
  • retirement and transmission realization rules;
  • geographic, land, environmental, or policy exclusions supplied by the study;
  • operational-template availability; and
  • any approved fast deliverability screen.

The objective is a study assumption and must be versioned. Its weights must not be hidden in an agent prompt.

Multiple realizations

When siting is underdetermined, the engine should publish a RealizationSet rather than conceal the ambiguity. A set may contain the best solution under the declared objective plus deterministic alternatives generated with diversity constraints, for example different host buses, corridors, or geographic concentrations.

Each member records:

  • its parent CapacityPlan and input hashes;
  • complete candidate-to-unit-to-bus lineage;
  • method, version, parameters, solver, and tie-breaker;
  • objective components and binding constraints;
  • unresolved mappings, warnings, and confidence;
  • fast-screen results; and
  • why it differs from other members.

This supports two distinct scientific questions: whether a plan has at least one viable realization, and whether it is robust across plausible realizations.

Screening sequence

Expensive PCM solves should follow progressively stronger deterministic checks:

  1. schema, units, identity, and capacity conservation;
  2. topology, connectivity, and operational-template completeness;
  3. static or reduced network deliverability checks;
  4. selected stress-hour or reduced-chronology screening; and
  5. full approved PCM horizon.

A fast screen may reject an invalid realization, but only the holistic assessment contract determines what evidence is sufficient for the final study claim.

Feasibility and assessment are separate gates

PCM infeasibility is evidence, not a diagnosis. It may result from a genuinely inadequate plan, an impossible nodal realization, incompatible operational parameters, bad chronology, a contract error, or numerical failure. Conversely, a feasible deterministic PCM run does not by itself pass reliability, economics, policy, emissions, resilience, market-performance, or any other holistic assessment.

The framework must preserve five distinct outcomes:

  1. Invalid input or contract: the assessment must not start.
  2. Execution or numerical failure: no scientific feasibility conclusion is justified.
  3. Mathematical infeasibility: the formulated downstream problem has no feasible solution under the pinned inputs and settings. This creates a diagnostic finding and may trigger feedback.
  4. Feasible but not accepted: the model solved, but one or more declared assessment criteria failed.
  5. Accepted: model feasibility and every required assessment gate passed.

The HolisticAssessmentContract must be pinned before the first solve and separate at least these gates:

GateExample evidence
Artifact integrityValid schemas, hashes, identities, units, calendars, and parent lineage
Realization fidelityZonal decisions conserved; every physical asset and network change reconciled
Model solvabilityAccepted solver termination and feasible primal solution
Optional operational adequacyUnserved energy, reserve shortfall, overload, curtailment, ramping, storage, and policy thresholds when an operational module is selected
Assessment-specific resultsThe metrics, horizons, thresholds, and evaluator required by each selected assessment module
Optional reliabilityStudy-defined deterministic contingencies or probabilistic metrics such as LOLE/EUE, only when a suitable reliability evaluator is selected
Scientific governanceNo unresolved high-severity findings; all material changes approved; final audit passed

The criterion, horizon, and tolerance belong in the contract. An agent must not make an infeasible or failed assessment pass by silently relaxing a threshold, enabling load shedding, changing a penalty, disabling a constraint, or shortening the evaluation horizon.

Diagnosis and revision routing

Every failed gate should first create a structured Finding. A diagnosis step then assigns the narrowest responsible layer. The workflow should try the lowest-authority scientifically valid change before modifying an upstream model.

Failure classTypical evidenceFirst responsible rolePermitted first response
Contract or data errorMissing IDs, incompatible units, incomplete chronology, stale parent hashArtifact owner / human data stewardCorrect or replace the invalid artifact; do not reinterpret it conversationally
Numerical or software failureSolver error, numerical instability, resource exhaustionModel agent plus runtime operatorRetry only under an approved numerical policy; otherwise stop for review
Mathematical infeasibilitySolver establishes infeasibility under the pinned formulation and inputsAssessment-model agent, then revision routerPublish available conflict, constraint-group, slack, or feasibility-relaxation diagnostics without treating the relaxation as an accepted solution
Realization-localBus overload, implausible concentration, site-cap violation, invalid unitization with zonal totals otherwise adequateRealization agentPropose a new policy or alternative mapping while preserving the CapacityPlan
PCM-assumption-localMissing or inappropriate operating template, initial state, outage, reserve eligibility, or chronology inputPCM agentCorrect an owned operational assumption within its approved range
Planning-structuralPersistent firm-capacity, flexibility, storage-duration, interface, or resource-location deficiency across defensible realizationsGTEP agentPropose a new planning constraint, candidate, cost, capacity credit, reserve rule, or scenario assumption
Assessment-policy conflictA selected assessment standard cannot be evaluated or met under the authorized model scope or budgetStudy agent and humanClarify the research question, approve expanded scope, or stop without a false-safe conclusion

When supported, an infeasibility diagnosis should retain an irreducible inconsistent subsystem, conflict refinement, violated constraint groups, or a separately labeled minimum-slack diagnostic. A feasibility relaxation is diagnostic evidence only; it must never overwrite or be reported as the original model solution.

The router should use evidence across several realizations before blaming GTEP. For example, one congested bus assignment is a realization problem; repeated congestion across diverse defensible assignments may justify a planning-level transmission or location constraint.

Revision contract

Agents may propose changes; deterministic tools apply approved changes. No agent may directly mutate an accepted parent artifact. Every revision creates a child scenario and a machine-readable delta.

A RevisionRequest should include fields similar to:

artifact_type: HOPE.revision_request
schema_version: 1
request_id: revision-uuid
study_id: future-2035
iteration: 2
finding_ids: [finding-017, finding-021]
requested_owner: gtep
classification: planning_structural
parent_artifacts:
  capacity_plan: {artifact_id: plan-001, sha256: "..."}
  realization_set: {artifact_id: realization-set-003, sha256: "..."}
  assessment_result: {artifact_id: pcm-assessment-006, sha256: "..."}
evidence:
  metric: reserve_shortfall_mwh
  observed: 418.2
  threshold: 0.0
  scope: "July peak and ramping hours; all three approved realizations"
proposed_changes:
  - path: planning.reserve_requirement.upward
    before: 0.07
    after: 0.09
    rationale: "..."
expected_effect: "increase planning procurement of flexible capacity"
alternatives_considered: ["additional storage duration", "interface expansion"]
approval_class: material_planning_assumption
status: proposed

The request must distinguish an observed result from an inferred cause and a proposed remedy. The GTEP agent may accept, reject, or replace the proposal with another documented change; it must not treat a PCM agent's inference as an authoritative planning instruction.

RoleMay doMust not do without escalation

Other assessment agentRun a selected reliability, market, policy, emissions, economics, or stress-test module and publish a typed AssessmentResultClaim authority outside its declared module or alter upstream artifacts

Agent knowledge should be explicit and reviewable: model documentation, formulation and data schemas, diagnostic playbooks, approved change templates, case provenance, and prior validated findings. Conversational memory is not a substitute for those sources.

Human-in-the-loop policy

Human approval should be required when a revision changes any of the following:

  • policy targets, assessment or reliability thresholds, demand, fuel or technology costs;
  • planning reserve or capacity-credit methodology;
  • candidate technologies, sites, network topology, or external data source;
  • model formulation rather than an allowlisted case parameter;
  • evaluation horizon or accepted data quality;
  • an established compute or iteration budget; or
  • the final publication decision after unresolved warnings.

Routine retries, status checks, deterministic transformations, and changes within an explicitly approved parameter envelope need not require repeated human approval.

Iteration, convergence, and stopping

An adaptive study is a lineage of immutable attempts, not one case directory edited repeatedly:

SPECIFIED -> GTEP_SOLVED -> REALIZED -> PCM_SOLVED -> VERIFIED
                                                |          |
                                                |          +-> ACCEPTED
                                                |
                                                +-> REVISION_REQUESTED
                                                          |
                         APPROVED_CHILD_SCENARIO <---------+

The workflow converges only when all hard gates in the pinned acceptance contract pass and no high-severity finding remains unresolved. A lower cost or smaller violation is not convergence if any hard selected assessment criterion still fails.

It must stop safely when any of these occurs:

  • acceptance;
  • the approved iteration or compute budget is exhausted;
  • no authorized revision addresses the findings;
  • successive iterations cycle or fail to make declared progress;
  • the necessary change exceeds an agent's authority; or
  • a human pauses or terminates the study.

The terminal result may be ACCEPTED, NOT_ACCEPTED, or INCONCLUSIVE. INCONCLUSIVE is preferable to silently weakening the criterion.

Recommended first vertical slice

The first scientific-agent implementation should be deliberately narrower than the final architecture:

  1. Freeze the current explicit-bus composite tool as the reference baseline.
  2. Define version-1 CapacityPlan, CandidateSiteCatalog, RealizationPolicy, RealizationSet, AssessmentResult, Finding, RevisionRequest, and HolisticAssessmentContract schemas.
  3. Implement one deterministic computed-siting method alongside explicit_bus; do not remove the validated method.
  4. Generate a small number of diverse nodal realizations for one zonal GTEP plan and run fast deliverability screens before July PCM.
  5. Support two feedback routes:
    • realization-local resiting with unchanged zonal totals; and
    • one reviewed planning-structural request, such as additional firm/flexible capacity or an interface constraint.
  6. Limit the loop to three iterations and require human approval for every GTEP assumption change.
  7. Evaluate whether the agents diagnose the correct layer, preserve provenance, avoid unauthorized changes, and improve acceptance—not whether additional conversational roles appear productive.

July PCM can establish the first operational feasibility and adequacy workflow. Its infeasibility and feasible-but-failed paths are both valid feedback-loop tests. It must not be labeled a complete probabilistic reliability study. Later milestones can compose annual or rolling operation, contingency analysis, EREC, DART, policy, emissions, economics, resilience, or another validated assessment module under its own contract.

What not to build

  • Do not let agents edit shared CSV files in place.
  • Do not pass capacity plans or PCM results only through natural-language messages.
  • Do not let the PCM agent directly change GTEP assumptions.
  • Do not treat one arbitrary nodal allocation as the unique physical truth.
  • Do not equate solver feasibility with successful holistic assessment.
  • Do not let an agent relax a threshold in order to declare success.
  • Do not rerun indefinitely; revisions require lineage, budgets, and stopping rules.
  • Do not add agent roles where a deterministic validator or optimizer is the appropriate mechanism.

Design outcome

The target is not three autonomous chatbots and not one fixed reliability pipeline. It is an adaptive scientific framework in which specialized agents diagnose and propose, deterministic HOPE tools transform and solve, typed assessment modules evaluate different study questions, immutable artifacts communicate, an independent verifier judges evidence, and humans retain authority over material scientific changes.
